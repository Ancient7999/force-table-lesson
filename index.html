Force table

Three strings pull on one small ring. Two of them carry known weights at chosen angles; the third is adjusted until the ring sits centred and the thread above it hangs straight. That third force, the equilibrant, is equal and opposite to the resultant of the other two, which is then found again from the formula and from a scale drawing.

Medical perspectives

Force balance in joints and muscles

A joint at rest is in equilibrium: the muscle pulls, the weight of the limb and the joint reaction add to zero as vectors, exactly like the three strings on the ring. Knowing two of them gives the third.

Biomechanics and physiotherapy

Traction set-ups use ropes, pulleys and weights to pull a limb in a chosen direction. The pull actually applied is the vector sum of the rope tensions, worked out just as on the force table.

Posture and movement

Standing still means the line of the total weight falls inside the base of support. Analysing posture and gait breaks body forces into components and adds them again, the same vector addition used here.

Lab 12 · TheoryVectors

Adding forces

Scalars and vectors

A mass of 0.20 kg is a scalar: a number with a unit. A force is a vector: it has a size and a direction, and two forces cannot be added by adding their sizes unless they point the same way. 2 N + 2 N can be anything from 0 N (opposite) to 4 N (together).

Head to tail: the triangle law

Draw F1 to scale. From its tip draw F2 to the same scale, in its own direction. The arrow from the tail of F1 to the tip of F2 is the resultant FR: one force that does the same job as the two together.

The angle θ is the angle between the directions of F1 and F2, measured from the line of F1 extended. The angle inside the triangle, between the two drawn sides, is 180° − θ.

The parallelogram law

The same result, drawn differently: put both forces tail to tail, complete the parallelogram, and the diagonal from the common point is FR. The upper half of the parallelogram is exactly the head-to-tail triangle.

The formula

FR = √( F1² + F2² + 2 F1 F2 cos θ )
  1. In the triangle, the sides are F1 and F2 and the angle between them is 180° − θ.
    law of cosines: FR² = F1² + F2² − 2F1F2 cos(180° − θ)
  2. cos(180° − θ) = − cos θ, so the minus sign turns into a plus.
    FR² = F1² + F2² + 2F1F2 cos θ
  3. Direction of the resultant, measured from F1:
    tan α = F2 sin θ ÷ (F1 + F2 cos θ)

Explore

At θ = 0 the forces simply add; at 180° they subtract; at 90° the formula becomes Pythagoras.

Equilibrium and the equilibrant

The ring is in equilibrium when the three string forces add to zero. Drawn head to tail they close into a triangle with no gap.

So the third force F3 has the same size as FR and points the opposite way, 180° from it. It is called the equilibrant, and on the table its size is simply m3g.

Why each step matters

Level table

On a tilted top the ring slides downhill on its own, adding a fake fourth force. The spirit level removes it.

Hanger mass

Each hanger has its own mass, 50 g here. The force is the whole hanging mass times g: hanger plus slotted masses.

Ring centred

The angles are read at the rim as if every string started at the centre. That is only true when the ring sits on the centre.

Friction

Pulley friction lets a small unbalanced force hide. Tapping the table gently before reading lets the ring settle where the forces really balance.

Sources of error

  • Friction in the pulleys, so a range of m3 appears to balance.
  • Reading the angles to the nearest degree; strings not exactly radial when the ring is off-centre.
  • Forgetting the mass of the hanger, or a mass label that is slightly off.
  • In the drawing: the chosen scale, pencil thickness, and reading the ruler and protractor.
Lab 12 · MethodProcedure

Procedure on the table

Apparatus

Steps

Good practice
  • Include the mass of each hanger in m.
  • Keep the strings horizontal over the table and running over the middle of each pulley.
  • Tap the table lightly before deciding the ring is centred, so that pulley friction does not hide a small imbalance.
  • Read each angle at the string, with the eye directly above the scale.
  • For the drawing, choose a scale that makes the triangle as large as the paper allows.
Care
  • Add masses gently; a sudden jerk can throw the ring off the pin or the string off a pulley.
  • Keep feet clear of the hangers.

Common slips

Hanger left out

0.15 kg of slotted masses on a 50 g hanger is m = 0.20 kg, not 0.15 kg.

Grams in F = mg

200 × 9.8 = 1960 is not a force in newtons. Convert first: 0.200 kg × 9.8 = 1.96 N.

Wrong angle

θ is the angle between F₁ and F₂ (60°), not the inside angle of the triangle (120°) and not the position of the third pulley.

Calculator in radians

cos 60 in radians is −0.95. In degrees it is 0.5. Check the mode before the square root.

Square root forgotten

14.65 is FR² in N². FR = √14.65 = 3.83 N.

Scale back-to-front

At 1 cm = 0.5 N, a 7.6 cm line is 7.6 × 0.5 = 3.80 N, not 7.6 ÷ 0.5.
